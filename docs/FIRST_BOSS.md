# Chapter I — The Bell That Would Not Mourn

This is the intended encounter screenplay and production target. Check the playable build for the subset currently implemented; this document is not a claim that every beat already exists.

## Arrival

A fixed isometric view looks down on a rain-soaked shrine courtyard. Empty offering bowls fill with water. An enormous cracked bell hangs beyond the fighting space. Its clapper moves without sound.

Ren enters from the lower steps. His wet footprints briefly darken the stones. A single warm lantern is the only steady light. Above it, a strip of memorial paper carries an erased name.

The camera closes slowly. Ren stops short of a kneeling armoured figure. The figure lifts his head before Ren's hand reaches the sword.

**REN:** “I came for the names.”

**KURODA:** “Then you came too late.”

**REN:** “You taught me to count the living.”

**KURODA:** “And you still refuse to count the cost.”

Kuroda stands. His coat remains motionless in the wind. The rain has been passing through it.

## Reveal: a missing sound

The rain audio cuts away. Falling drops suspend for a fraction of a second. A thin gold fracture crosses the screen along the bell's crack, splitting the composition rather than covering it with a conventional warning card.

The name **KURODA** appears in widely spaced ivory letters. Beneath it: **THE HOLLOW WARDEN**. A smaller line reads: **The man who taught you to survive.**

The bell's first audible strike arrives after the title disappears. The camera returns to the playable angle as Kuroda draws his sword. The player receives control before he advances; the reveal must never conceal an incoming hit.

## Phase one: the lesson

Kuroda fights like someone who knows Ren's training. He uses three legible attacks: a broad approaching cut, a committed straight lunge, and a delayed ground strike that punishes panic dodges. Telegraphs originate from his body and continue onto the ground. Colour supports shape; it must not be the sole warning.

He leaves real recovery windows. A successful deflection produces a brief silence, an ivory contact line, and a restrained hit pause. The satisfying moment is seeing the old teacher lose balance.

Optional combat lines, used sparingly: “Your feet. Again.” / “Still watching the blade.” / Ren, after a deflection: “I remember.”

## Phase two: those beneath the stone

At half health, the fight pauses safely. Kuroda catches Ren's next blow with a bare hand. His armour separates at its bindings. Inside are folded memorial strips, each written with a name that has been crossed out.

**KURODA:** “They are not buried beneath us.”

The bell sways. Gold lines spread through the courtyard joints.

**KURODA:** “They are holding me up.”

His silhouette expands as strips of paper draw the shoulder plates apart. The transformation changes the actual encounter: a strike now leaves a delayed echo, teaching the player to dodge the second consequence rather than punish immediately. Avoid turning the phase into an unreadable particle storm.

Ren realises Kuroda has been containing the stolen names inside his body. This does not erase his responsibility. He sealed the road and helped fill the bell. His vigil is punishment he appointed for himself because it was easier than facing the survivors.

## Victory: an unfinished name

The final blow breaks the bindings instead of dismembering Kuroda. Papers settle into the rain. He kneels in the same place where Ren found him.

**KURODA:** “Your sister rang the bell.”

**REN:** “She was here?”

**KURODA:** “She knew what it would cost.”

The bell splits. Inside it are hundreds of etched names. Aki's is unfinished, a groove interrupted before the final stroke. Someone stopped the taking of her name. Her fate remains uncertain.

Ren turns Kuroda's sword flat and uses it to shelter the nearest memorial strip from the rain. No declaration of forgiveness. No victory quip.

**REN:** “Then I will finish nothing for them.”

The chapter card reads **A NAME REMAINS**. The player can replay the duel or read the recovered testimony. The reward is a lead toward the Quiet Accord's foundry, not proof that Ren's sister is alive.

## Production order

1. **Playable boss prototype:** responsive ground movement, sword, dodge, deflection, readable enemy timings, health, defeat/retry, phase transition, and victory. In-engine introduction and phase scene are skippable. Keep the arena bounded and the camera fixed during active combat.
2. **Character pass:** Ren's proportions, four-direction poses, sword arcs, foot placement, and recovery animations. Kuroda must share enough technique with Ren to imply a relationship before dialogue.
3. **Cinematic pass:** improve staging, rain interruption, bell fracture, letter animation, sound timing, and post-fight paper. Add reduced-motion and flash controls alongside effects rather than after them.
4. **Encounter expansion:** two short PvE scenes on the approach, each introducing one boss lesson; then a direct boss-rematch option. Do not create a second mode or progression economy yet.
5. **Browser release:** keyboard and pointer plus touch controls; test Chrome, Firefox, Safari, and Edge on defined supported versions. Profile ordinary devices, keep assets small, and verify muted play, resizing, tab suspension, and retries.
6. **Next protagonist:** Eleanor's guard mechanics and archive encounter. Build one complete dramatic fight per character before building a campaign map.

Treat cinematic spectacle as choreography: anticipation, a precise impact, and enough quiet to feel its consequence. Every effect should reveal threat, character, or story. The first release does not require voice acting, video cutscenes, multiplayer, a backend, or a large download.
